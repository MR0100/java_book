# C14 — Mock Interview Library

The rest of L6 teaches you the *material* — DSA, system design, behavioral frameworks, the staff-level question banks. This chapter shows you what it actually **sounds like in the room**: full, verbatim-style mock interviews, turn by turn, with a strong-but-human candidate, the interviewer's hidden rubric exposed, inline coaching at every decision point, and an honest scorecard at the end. Reading a transcript is the closest thing to sitting in on a real loop.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> These are **representative mocks**, not leaked or proprietary questions. Company names (Amazon, Google, Meta, Stripe, Razorpay, Goldman-style) denote a well-known *interview archetype* — the format, bar, and signals that company is known for — so you can prepare for the *style*. The candidate is deliberately strong-but-imperfect: they stumble and recover, because that is more instructive than a flawless script.

## How To Use This Chapter

1. **Read the transcript first, cover the commentary.** Try to predict what the interviewer is scoring before you read the callouts.
2. **Study the debrief.** Each mock ends with a scorecard against the rubric and the two or three changes that would have raised the verdict.
3. **Run the variations.** Each mock lists what-if branches; rehearse answering those out loud.
4. **Then do the Practice.** Redo the round yourself, on a timer, speaking aloud.

## What You'll Learn

- Hear the cadence of a real loop: clarifying questions, thinking aloud, recovering from a wrong turn, and closing a problem.
- See the **hidden rubric** each round scores against, so you optimize for *signal*, not just a correct answer.
- Internalize what strong vs. weak looks like — including a full chapter of verbatim *bad* answers (T15) and their fixes.
- Carry a repeatable structure into every round: coding, design, behavioral, managerial, negotiation.
